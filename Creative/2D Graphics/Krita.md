Krita is a free, open-source painting program built for digital artists, illustrators, comic creators, and concept artists. It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux and is designed around drawing and painting rather than photo editing.

Website: [https://krita.org/en/download/](https://krita.org/en/download/)

---

## What Is Krita?

Krita focuses on the experience of painting. It ships with a large library of brush presets, supports pen tablets with pressure and tilt sensitivity, and includes tools that artists actually use day to day: stabilizers, perspective guides, and a brush editor with deep customization. It also includes frame-by-frame 2D animation tools.

It is commonly used for:

- Digital painting and concept art
- Character design and illustration
- Comics and manga
- Textures and matte painting
- Simple 2D animation

---

## Getting Started

1. Download Krita from the official website (also available on Steam and the Microsoft Store).
2. Launch the program and choose **File → New**.
3. Pick a canvas size, for example 2000 × 2000 px at 300 DPI for print-quality work.
4. If you use a drawing tablet, install its drivers first and test pressure sensitivity in **Settings → Configure Krita → Tablet Settings**.

## The Interface

| Area | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Toolbox** (left) | Brush, eraser, selection, transform, and fill tools |
| **Canvas** (center) | Your painting surface |
| **Brush presets** (right or popup) | Pick and organize brushes |
| **Layers docker** | Manage layers, blend modes, and opacity |
| **Color selector** | Choose colors via wheel, triangle, or palette |
| **Tool options** | Settings for the active tool |

Dockers can be rearranged, so you can build a workspace that fits your style.

---

## Key Concepts

- **Brush engines** – different brush behaviors such as pixel, color smudge, shape, and clone.
- **Brush presets** – ready-made brushes for pencil, ink, watercolor, oil, and airbrush effects.
- **Layers and blend modes** – build up artwork in stages (sketch, line art, colors, shading) and control how layers interact.
- **Layer types** – paint layers, vector layers, group layers, and filter layers.
- **Stabilizers** – smooth shaky lines for cleaner inking.
- **Drawing assistants** – rulers, perspective grids, and vanishing-point tools for accurate geometry.
- **Animation tools** – a timeline and onion-skinning for frame-by-frame animation.

## Example Walkthrough

Painting a simple character illustration:

1. Create a new 2000 × 2000 px canvas at 300 DPI.
2. Sketch the pose on a new layer using a pencil brush, keeping lines loose.
3. Lower the sketch layer's opacity and add a new layer for clean line art, using the stabilizer for smoother strokes.
4. Add a layer beneath the line art for flat base colors.
5. Use **Alpha Lock** or a clipping group to paint shading and highlights without leaving the shapes.
6. Add a background on a separate layer and adjust the overall mood using a filter layer such as Color Balance.
7. Save as `.kra` to keep all layers.
8. Export with **File → Export** as PNG for sharing.

---

## Tips and Common Mistakes

- **Work large, then scale down.** Starting at a high resolution gives you room for detail.
- **Separate your layers** (sketch, lines, color, shading) so changes stay easy.
- **Build a small set of favorite brushes** rather than constantly browsing hundreds.
- **Use the Color Picker** (`Ctrl + click`) to stay consistent with your palette.
- **Save a `.kra` master** and export copies, as flattened formats lose your layers.

---

## When To Choose Krita

Choose Krita when your main goal is drawing or painting. For photo retouching and pixel-level compositing, [GIMP](./Gimp.md) is the better choice, and for scalable logos and icons, use [Adobe Illustrator](./AdobeIllustrator.md).
