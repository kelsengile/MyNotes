Adobe Premiere Pro is a professional non-linear video editor used for everything from YouTube videos to feature films. It is paid, subscription-based software for Windows and macOS (with a free trial).

Website: [https://www.adobe.com/products/premiere.html](https://www.adobe.com/products/premiere.html)

---

## What Is Premiere Pro?

Premiere Pro lets you import footage, arrange clips on a timeline, trim and cut them, then add audio, titles, color, and effects. It works smoothly with After Effects, Photoshop, and Audition, so assets can move back and forth without exporting and re-importing.

It is commonly used for:

- YouTube and social media videos
- Documentaries, interviews, and corporate videos
- Short films and feature films
- Multi-camera editing for events and podcasts
- Adding captions and subtitles

---

## Getting Started

1. Install **Adobe Creative Cloud** and download Premiere Pro (a free trial is available).
2. Create a new project with **File → New → Project** and give it a name and location.
3. Import media with **File → Import** (or `Ctrl/Cmd + I`) or drag files into the Project panel.
4. Drag a clip onto the timeline area to automatically create a **Sequence** that matches its settings.

## The Interface

| Area | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Project panel** | Stores and organizes imported media in bins |
| **Source monitor** | Preview and mark clips before adding them |
| **Program monitor** | Preview the current sequence |
| **Timeline** | Arrange clips on video and audio tracks |
| **Tools panel** | Selection, razor, ripple, slip, and other editing tools |
| **Effects / Essential Graphics / Lumetri** | Effects, titles, and color controls |
| **Audio meters** | Monitor volume and prevent clipping |

---

## Key Concepts

- **Project panel and bins** – organize imported footage, audio, and graphics into folders.
- **Sequences and timeline** – the editing workspace where clips are arranged in order.
- **Tracks** – layers of video (V1, V2…) and audio (A1, A2…) that stack on the timeline.
- **Ripple and roll edits** – trim clips while keeping the sequence intact.
- **Lumetri Color** – built-in color correction and grading tools.
- **Essential Graphics** – create and customize titles and lower thirds.
- **Proxies** – lightweight copies of high-resolution footage that make editing smoother.

## Useful Shortcuts

| Shortcut | Action |
|---|---|
| `V` | Selection tool |
| `C` | Razor tool (cut clips) |
| `J` / `K` / `L` | Play reverse / pause / play forward |
| `I` / `O` | Set in point / out point |
| `Ctrl/Cmd + K` | Cut at playhead |
| `Shift + Delete` | Ripple delete |
| `Ctrl/Cmd + M` | Open Export settings |

## Example Walkthrough

Editing a short interview video:

1. Create a new project and import your interview footage, b-roll, and music.
2. Drag the main interview clip onto a new **Sequence**.
3. Use the Razor tool (`C`) or `Ctrl/Cmd + K` to cut out mistakes, then ripple delete the gaps.
4. Place b-roll on **V2** above the interview to cover cuts.
5. Add a **Cross Dissolve** between clips from the **Effects** panel.
6. Place music on an audio track and lower its volume under the dialogue.
7. Use **Lumetri Color** to correct exposure and white balance.
8. Add a title using the Type tool or **Essential Graphics**.
9. Use **File → Export → Media** and choose H.264 for a web-ready MP4.

## Tips and Common Mistakes

- **Organize your media early** with bins and clear names.
- **Use proxies** when editing 4K or heavy footage on a modest computer.
- **Watch your audio meters** and avoid peaking above 0 dB.
- **Save often** and rely on Auto Save, which keeps timestamped backups.
- **Don't overuse transitions.** Straight cuts are usually the cleanest choice.

---

## When To Choose Premiere Pro

Choose Premiere Pro when you work in the Adobe ecosystem or collaborate with teams that do. For heavy color work or a free all-in-one option, [DaVinci Resolve](./DaVinciResolve.md) is a strong alternative. For complex animated graphics, build them in [Adobe After Effects](../Motion%20Graphics/AdobeAfterEffects.md) and bring them into your edit.
