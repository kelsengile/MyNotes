# Lesson 35 - Decentralized Exchanges & AMMs

## 35.1 Order Books vs. Automated Market Makers

Traditional exchanges match buyers and sellers through an order book. Most decentralized exchanges (DEXs) instead use an **Automated Market Maker (AMM)** model, where trades happen against a shared pool of funds (a liquidity pool) rather than a matching counterparty, with prices determined algorithmically based on the pool's reserves.

---

## 35.2 The Constant Product Formula

The most common AMM design, popularized by Uniswap, prices assets using the formula `x * y = k`, where `x` and `y` are the reserves of two tokens in a pool and `k` is held constant across trades. As a trader buys more of one token, its reserve shrinks and its price rises along a curve, ensuring the pool never fully runs out of either asset.

```
reserveA * reserveB = k (constant)
```

---

## 35.3 Liquidity Providers

Anyone can become a **liquidity provider (LP)** by depositing a pair of tokens into a pool, receiving LP tokens representing their share, and earning a portion of trading fees generated by that pool. In exchange for these fees, LPs take on **impermanent loss** — a reduction in value (relative to simply holding the tokens) that occurs when the price ratio between the two pooled tokens shifts significantly.

---

## 35.4 Slippage and Price Impact

Because AMM prices move along a curve as trades happen, larger trades relative to pool size cause more **price impact** — the difference between the expected and executed price. To protect against this (and against front-running, see Lesson 27), DEX interfaces let users set a maximum acceptable **slippage** percentage, causing the transaction to revert if the price moves unfavorably beyond that threshold before execution.
