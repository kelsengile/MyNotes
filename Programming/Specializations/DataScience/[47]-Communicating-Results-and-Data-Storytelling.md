## 47.1 Why Communication Matters as Much as Analysis

A brilliant analysis that nobody understands or acts on has no impact. Communicating results clearly to non-technical stakeholders — explaining what was found, why it matters, and what to do about it — is often what separates data science work that actually changes decisions from work that sits unread in a report.

---

## 47.2 Know Your Audience

Tailor the level of technical detail to who's listening:

- **Executives** — want the bottom line and the recommended action first, with technical detail available only if asked.
- **Fellow analysts/data scientists** — want methodology, assumptions, and caveats in more depth.
- **General stakeholders** — benefit from plain language, concrete examples, and visuals over statistical jargon.

A good habit: lead with the conclusion, then support it — not the reverse.

---

## 47.3 Effective Data Visualization for Communication

Explanatory charts (as opposed to the quick exploratory charts from Lesson 7) should be deliberately simplified to make one clear point:

- Remove unnecessary chart clutter (gridlines, extra colors, 3D effects that distort perception).
- Use color intentionally — to highlight the one data point that matters, not just for decoration.
- Choose the chart type that best fits the message (Lesson 7.4) — a well-chosen simple chart beats an impressive but confusing one.
- Add a clear, plain-language title that states the takeaway, not just the chart's contents (e.g. "Revenue grew 20% after the redesign" rather than "Revenue by Month").

---

## 47.4 Structuring a Data Story

A common and effective structure:

1. **Context** — what question were we answering, and why did it matter?
2. **Finding** — what did we discover, stated clearly and concisely?
3. **Evidence** — the key chart(s) and numbers supporting the finding.
4. **Recommendation** — what should be done as a result?
5. **Caveats** — what limitations or uncertainty should the audience be aware of (tying back to the confidence intervals and significance testing from Lessons 18-19)?

This structure keeps a report focused on decisions and action, rather than becoming a dump of every analysis performed along the way.
