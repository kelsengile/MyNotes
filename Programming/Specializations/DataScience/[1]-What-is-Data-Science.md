## 1.1 What is Data Science?

Data Science is the practice of turning raw data into understanding and decisions. It sits at the intersection of three skill areas:

- **Statistics & Math** — understanding uncertainty, patterns, and relationships in numbers.
- **Programming** — collecting, cleaning, and transforming data at scale.
- **Domain Knowledge** — knowing what questions matter and how to interpret results correctly.

A data scientist typically moves through a repeatable cycle: ask a question, gather data, explore and clean it, model it, and communicate the findings so someone can act on them.

---

## 1.2 Common Roles in Data Science

The field is broad, and titles vary between companies, but a few common roles include:

- **Data Analyst** — focuses on descriptive analysis, dashboards, and reporting on what already happened.
- **Data Scientist** — builds statistical and machine learning models to predict or explain outcomes.
- **Machine Learning Engineer** — takes models from research code to production systems.
- **Data Engineer** — builds the pipelines and infrastructure that move and store data reliably.
- **MLOps Engineer** — keeps deployed models running, monitored, and up to date.

Many people wear several of these hats at once, especially at smaller companies.

---

## 1.3 The Data Science Workflow

Most data science projects follow a version of this loop, which later lessons in this course each dig into:

1. **Define the problem** — what decision or question are we trying to support?
2. **Collect data** — pull from files, databases, APIs, or the web (Lessons 8-9).
3. **Clean and wrangle** — handle missing values, fix inconsistent formats (Lessons 10-11).
4. **Explore** — summarize and visualize to find patterns (Lessons 12-15).
5. **Model** — apply statistics or machine learning (Lessons 16-37).
6. **Deploy and monitor** — put the model to use and keep it healthy (Lessons 38-41).
7. **Communicate** — explain results clearly to stakeholders (Lesson 47).

This is rarely a straight line — it's normal to loop back to earlier steps as you learn more about the data.

---

## 1.4 Why Data Science Matters

Organizations use data science to make decisions grounded in evidence rather than intuition alone: forecasting demand, detecting fraud, personalizing recommendations, diagnosing equipment failures, and more. The tools in this course apply across industries — the underlying skills (clean data, sound statistics, careful modeling, honest communication) transfer everywhere.
