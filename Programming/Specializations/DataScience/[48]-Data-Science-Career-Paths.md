## 48.1 Common Career Tracks

Building on the roles introduced in Lesson 1, here's how these tracks tend to develop over a career:

- **Data Analyst -> Senior Analyst / Analytics Manager** — deepening expertise in reporting, dashboards, and business-facing insight, often moving toward leading an analytics team.
- **Data Scientist -> Senior/Staff Data Scientist** — taking on more ambiguous, higher-impact modeling problems and increasing technical depth in statistics and ML.
- **ML Engineer -> ML Platform/Infrastructure Engineer** — focusing on the systems that let many models be trained, deployed, and monitored reliably at scale.
- **Individual Contributor -> Management** — some data scientists move into leading teams of analysts, data scientists, and engineers rather than continuing to build models hands-on.

---

## 48.2 Building a Portfolio

Since much of data science work is judged by demonstrated ability, a strong portfolio matters, especially early in a career:

- Complete end-to-end projects (using the workflow from Lesson 1) with real or realistic datasets, not just isolated exercises.
- Host code on GitHub with clear documentation (Lesson 3) so others can understand and run it.
- Write up findings with the communication practices from Lesson 47 — a project explained clearly is worth more than one that only you understand.
- Focus depth over breadth — two or three well-executed, clearly explained projects tend to impress more than a dozen shallow ones.

---

## 48.3 Continuing to Learn

Data science is a fast-moving field. Productive ways to keep learning after this course:

- Follow real research and industry blog posts on new techniques (particularly in deep learning and LLMs, Lessons 31-37, which evolve quickly).
- Participate in data competitions (e.g. Kaggle) to practice applying skills to new, unfamiliar datasets.
- Read post-mortems and case studies of real deployed ML systems to learn how MLOps and monitoring (Lessons 40-41) play out in practice.
- Specialize deliberately over time — most experienced practitioners develop deep expertise in one or two areas (e.g. NLP, computer vision, causal inference) rather than staying general forever.

---

## 48.4 Closing Thoughts

This course walked through the full data science workflow: setting up tools, collecting and cleaning data, exploring it statistically, building models from simple regressions to deep neural networks and transformers, deploying and monitoring them responsibly, and communicating results clearly. The path from here is mostly practice — working through real, messy datasets end to end is what turns these individual lessons into genuine skill. Good luck, and happy learning!
