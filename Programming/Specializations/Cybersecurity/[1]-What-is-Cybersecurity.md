# Lesson 1 - What is Cybersecurity? Careers & Domains Overview

## 1.1 What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications, and data from unauthorized access, damage, theft, or disruption. It combines technology, processes, and people: firewalls and encryption are worthless if an employee shares their password over the phone, and the best-trained staff can't stop an attacker if the network has no controls at all.

Cybersecurity is usually split into two broad perspectives:

- **Defensive (Blue Team)** — protecting systems: hardening configurations, monitoring for intrusions, responding to incidents.
- **Offensive (Red Team)** — thinking like an attacker: finding weaknesses before criminals do, through penetration testing and vulnerability research.

Both perspectives study the same attack techniques; they just apply that knowledge toward opposite goals. This course will introduce both.

---

## 1.2 Why Cybersecurity Matters

Nearly every organization now depends on digital systems to operate: banks move money electronically, hospitals store patient records digitally, and factories run on networked control systems. This dependence creates value for attackers to steal (data, money, intellectual property) and leverage for disruption (ransomware, sabotage).

The consequences of a security failure can include financial loss, legal liability, reputational damage, safety risks (in industrial or medical settings), and loss of customer trust. Because of this, cybersecurity is no longer treated as a purely technical concern — it is a business risk that executives and regulators actively manage.

---

## 1.3 Common Career Domains

Cybersecurity is a broad field, and most professionals specialize. Common domains include:

- **Security Operations (SOC Analyst)** — monitors alerts and investigates suspicious activity in real time.
- **Penetration Testing / Red Teaming** — simulates attacks to find exploitable weaknesses.
- **Incident Response & Digital Forensics** — investigates breaches after they occur and helps organizations recover.
- **Application Security (AppSec)** — reviews and secures software during development.
- **Cloud Security** — secures workloads and data hosted on cloud platforms.
- **Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC)** — manages policies, audits, and regulatory requirements.
- **Identity & Access Management (IAM)** — controls who can access what.
- **Security Engineering** — builds and maintains security tooling and infrastructure.

No single course covers every domain in depth, but understanding the fundamentals in this course prepares you to specialize in any of them later.

## 1.4 How This Course is Organized

This course moves from foundational concepts (the CIA triad, networking, operating systems) through specialized topics (cryptography, web security, malware) and into applied skills (offensive and defensive techniques, tools, governance). Each lesson builds on earlier ones, so it's recommended to go through them roughly in order, especially through the "Core Concepts" and "Networking Fundamentals" sections.
